Weaving here is locked to one active row at a time, bottom-up, the way a real vertical loom actually works — everything above the active row is bare, untouchable warp until you reach it. Hover a warp pair and click (or press A / S) to hand-tie one knot; there's no dragging a color across the board, every knot is its own discrete action. Once the active row has at least one knot, Pass weft (or press W) throws the horizontal thread loosely across it — only then can you Beat it down (or slam Spacebar) to lock the row tight and advance to the next one. Try to beat before wefting, or weft an empty row, and the loom refuses with a tension warning, same as skipping a step on a real one. Arm a motif thumbnail below to preview one documented shape's row-by-row slice on the active row only — it still never fills a cell for you, it just tells you which color that cell wants once you hand-tie it, background nodes included.

Warp — the loom's bare threads, never colored
Weft — the over-under pass that locks a finished row; fine city-workshop regions show it as a tighter double pass (depressed warp), village/nomadic regions as one looser pass
